# Corporate / Marketing Surface Calibration

This file captures **observed** patterns from the current official Filigran website. It is not a substitute for an internal brand book.

## Content architecture

The current homepage follows a clear B2B cybersecurity narrative:

1. concise top-level promise;
2. short explanation of platform value;
3. primary and secondary CTA;
4. trust evidence/customer logos;
5. problem framing with concrete metrics;
6. platform/capability explanation;
7. product proof and screenshots;
8. additional capabilities/toolbox;
9. metrics and customer testimony;
10. editorial/content proof;
11. community and integration ecosystem;
12. closing CTA.

Use this as a narrative rhythm when appropriate, not as a mandatory landing-page template.

## Voice

Observed traits:

- short decisive headlines;
- action verbs;
- emphasis on outcomes and operational clarity;
- technical terms used when meaningful, not as decoration;
- metrics used to substantiate value;
- open-source + enterprise credibility;
- "signal vs noise" / prioritization framing;
- ecosystem and integration depth.

For corporate copy, prefer specific claims that can be supported. Do not invent performance statistics or customer claims.

## Visual hierarchy

Observed corporate surfaces use:

- large, clean typographic statements;
- generous whitespace;
- strong contrast between white and dark/saturated sections;
- vivid blue/cyan/turquoise gradient moments;
- occasional indigo/violet in gradient transitions;
- polished screenshots and product imagery;
- logo rows and proof blocks;
- modular content cards for resources/use cases;
- clean rounded contemporary UI rather than cyberpunk chrome.

## Hero treatment

A Filigran-style hero should typically have:

- one strong statement;
- one concise proof/explanation paragraph;
- one or two clear actions;
- enough negative space to make the message feel confident;
- a branded gradient, diagram, or product visual with a functional storytelling role.

Do not put five competing cards above the fold.

## Product imagery

Use product screenshots as evidence of capability.

When presenting a screenshot:

- keep it legible enough to communicate real product maturity;
- crop around the feature being discussed;
- use subtle depth/gradient framing rather than fake terminal overlays;
- do not obscure the UI with decorative noise.

## Metrics

Large metrics are a recurring proof device.

Use them only when the data is sourced and the context is clear. Pair a number with a short explanation of what it measures.

## Ecosystem visuals

Integration/customer logo density is useful to communicate reach, but should come after the main message. Keep logo treatment neutral and consistent.

## Corporate CTA language

Prefer clear actions such as exploring a platform, trying a live demo, getting started, reading a resource, or booking a demo — chosen according to the artifact's actual goal.

Avoid cute or ambiguous CTA copy in enterprise/security contexts.

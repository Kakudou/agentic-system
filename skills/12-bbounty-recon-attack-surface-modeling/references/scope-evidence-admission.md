# Scope And Evidence Admission

## Purpose

Establish whether a supplied observation may be represented in the model. Admission controls model scope; it does not authorize collection, testing, disclosure, or remediation.

## Preconditions

- Written authorization identifies the program, permitted assets, dates, and constraints.
- Each evidence item has a source, observation time, collector or system context, and handling classification where available.

## Method

1. Record the authorization reference and its exact in-scope, excluded, and ambiguous asset rules.
2. Assign each submitted item an evidence ID and preserve its supplied locator or record reference.
3. Admit an item only when its asset association and collection context fit the authorization. Label indirect associations as unconfirmed.
4. Keep excluded and inadmissible material out of the model while recording the exclusion reason in the handoff.

## Interpretation

Admission means “eligible for synthesis,” not “true,” “owned,” or “safe to disclose.” A model may show an unknown relationship when evidence supports observation but not ownership or scope membership.

## False-Positive And Attribution Controls

- Do not treat a naming resemblance, shared provider, or historical association as ownership.
- Do not promote a single unattributed record into an asset claim.
- Preserve source time because stale observations can describe a former state.

## Privacy And Scope Limits

Do not retain credentials, personal data, secrets, full session material, or unnecessary payloads. Redact or reference approved secure evidence storage. Stop on missing authorization, asset ambiguity, or a request to expand scope.

## Evidence And Handoff

Include the authorization reference, admission decision, exclusions, and unresolved scope questions. The recipient decides whether clarification or further authorized work is needed.

# Scope-Source Admission

## Purpose and Preconditions

Determine whether a document or communication may define engagement scope. Require an identifiable source, its issuer or accountable owner, a retrieval date, and evidence that it applies to this engagement. A source without enough provenance is a question, not authorization.

## Documentation Methodology

Record a source ID, title, issuer, stable locator, version or effective date, retrieval date, and the exact quoted scope language. Preserve the supplied form and distinguish primary program policy from supporting material. Record supersession only when the source expressly establishes it or the authorized owner confirms it.

## Uncertainty and Scope Controls

Do not infer authority from branding, repository location, prior program practice, or an unsigned copy. Do not merge documents merely because their identifiers overlap. If source authority, freshness, applicability, or supersession is uncertain, preserve both records and stop for source-owner clarification. This reference admits evidence only; it does not authorize target expansion or active validation.

## Evidence and Handoff

Hand off the source register with verbatim quotations, locators, dates, and an admission status of `admitted`, `supporting`, or `unresolved`. State all unresolved provenance questions prominently.

## Authoritative Sources

- The engagement's current official scope or policy page.
- The signed statement of work, rules of engagement, or written authorization.
- Written clarification from the authorized program owner.

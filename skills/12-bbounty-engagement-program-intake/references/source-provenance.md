# Source Provenance

## Purpose

Establish which program materials support the intake packet and whether they are current enough to rely on.

## Preconditions

- At least one program document, URL, or platform record is available.
- The intake process can record access time and document identity.

## Documentation Methodology

1. Assign each source a source ID and record title, publisher, canonical location, access time, and visible version, date, or revision signal.
2. Prefer program-owned policy, scope, and legal documents. Record whether platform material is linked, incorporated, or merely contextual.
3. Capture only the excerpts needed to support each intake rule, using headings, table rows, page numbers, or stable anchors as locators.
4. Record inaccessible, dynamic, untranslated, or superseded material as an evidence gap.

## Interpretation And Uncertainty Controls

- Access time is not proof that a document was current or complete.
- Publication date, revision date, and last-access time are distinct facts.
- Do not rely on cached copies, search snippets, or summaries when an authoritative source is available.
- Conflicting revisions remain unresolved until the program clarifies which governs.

## Evidence And Handoff

Attach a source register to the intake packet and identify the primary source for each material rule. Handoff must call out every missing, stale, conflicting, or unverifiable source.

## Concise Sources

- Program-owned policy, scope, and terms are primary sources.
- NIST SP 800-161r1, Cybersecurity Supply Chain Risk Management Practices: <https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/161/r1/final>

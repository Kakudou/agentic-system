# Source Credibility And Provenance

## Purpose

Assess whether a source can support a bounded research claim and preserve enough provenance for independent review.

## Preconditions

- The source has been admitted and a stable locator or retained permitted excerpt is available.
- Its publisher, publication date or version, and access date can be recorded or marked unavailable.

## Documentation Methodology

Prefer primary sources such as official standards, vendor documentation, and issuer statements. Assess authority, independence, specificity, recency, version alignment, and whether the source distinguishes observation from interpretation. Record what was reviewed and the exact section, page, heading, or quoted passage used. Corroborate consequential claims with independent sources where feasible; do not treat repetition as independence.

## Provenance And Privacy Controls

Preserve attribution and use only the minimum excerpt needed for review. Do not redistribute restricted, copyrighted, confidential, personal, or credential-bearing material. Record a handling restriction instead of copying material that cannot be safely retained.

## Evidence And Handoff

Hand off the source record, credibility rationale, exact locator, access date, version state, handling restriction, and known gaps. A credible source informs research context; it does not establish applicability to a target.

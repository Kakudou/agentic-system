# Scope Admission

## Purpose And Preconditions

Decide whether a proposed planning item belongs to an explicit authorization boundary. Require the authorization reference, in-scope asset identifier, owner or program contact, time window, permitted methods, account/data restrictions, and exclusions.

## Planning Methodology

1. Transcribe the supplied boundary without broadening asset names, paths, identities, or methods.
2. Match each proposed planning item to one in-scope identifier and one authorization clause.
3. Record constraints that limit any later validation, including third-party services and production safeguards.
4. Mark the item admitted only when all material fields agree; otherwise mark it blocked pending clarification.

## Interpretation And Uncertainty

Authorization language can be incomplete or stale. Treat ambiguity, expired windows, conflicting program rules, and inferred subdomains or endpoints as out of scope until the owner confirms them. Scope admission authorizes planning, not execution.

## False-Positive And Scope Limits

An asset mention, technology observation, historical report, or public reference is not proof of ownership or permission. Do not infer scope from DNS relationships, branding, authentication access, or an accessible URL.

## Evidence And Handoff

Preserve the authorization reference, exact asset identifier, decision, constraints, open questions, and responsible contact. Send blocked or ambiguous items to the named scope owner; do not convert them into test tasks.

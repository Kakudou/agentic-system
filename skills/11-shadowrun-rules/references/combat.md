# Shadowrun Sixth World 6E — combat desk guide

**Read embedded full-book sections**: printed pp. 104–125, plus game concepts 34–52, gear 244–303, magic 126–169 and Matrix 170–195. The full-page Markdown text is in [the offline source index](offline-source-index.md).

1. Establish awareness, cover, distance, visibility, initiative, surprised parties, relevant ongoing states. Distinguish realistic physical consequences from abstract positions.
2. Determine combat rounds and turns; assign legal Major/Minor actions, any conversion of Minor to Major as allowed, movement, defensive options and timing. Faster individuals may have more Minor actions; do not simply give everybody the same unverified action allowance.
3. Choose attack skill and attribute appropriate to weapon or activity; validate range, ammo, attachments, fire mode, smartlink, recoil/position and environment. Determine **Attack Rating for the relevant range band** and **Defense Rating** including armor. Award or deny Edge using the correct comparison, side caps and circumstances.
4. Establish defender's dice pool and its modifiers; perform opposed attack test using the actual 6E tiebreaker and any Edge spent at legal time. Mark hits, net hits, glitch and critical glitch separately. Do not borrow the 5E formula from memory.
5. On a hit, apply base Damage Value and net-hit effect as applicable, damage type, special ammunition and resistance test; **Body is used for normal damage soak, not armor**. Maintain separate Physical/Stun tracks, overflow and wounds, stabilization and recovery. Do not quietly recover condition between scenes.
6. Update rounds, conditions, position, ammunition, services, alarms, reinforcements and retreat options; these become event facts only when actually resolved.

For grenades, explosions, elemental effects, armor modifiers, toxins, melee options, multiple attacks, vehicles, astral combat and special creature powers consult the exact appropriate source passage before resolution. Avoid 'quickly approximating' a specialist subsystem as a generic d20 check. Narrative approach remains action-first: players can attempt suppression, distraction, surrender, flanking, negotiation and escape, not only attack commands.

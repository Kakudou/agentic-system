# Public-Context Review

## Purpose

Use permitted public program context to identify duplicated effort, changed scope, or documentation that affects review order. It does not measure other researchers, novelty, or expected acceptance.

## Preconditions

- The program permits use of the source.
- The source is public, attributable, and relevant to the named program or asset.
- Its publication date and limits are recorded.

## Evidence-Led Method

1. Review official scope updates, known-issue notices, and public disclosures relevant to the hypothesis.
2. Record only concrete effects: an asset was removed from scope, a feature changed, a duplicate class is explicitly acknowledged, or a testing constraint was published.
3. Mark the hypothesis `defer` when the source leaves its current applicability unclear; do not assume the issue is fixed or already known.
4. Separate program statements from community commentary and label the latter as contextual only.

## Interpretation And Controls

- Do not use target size, program age, issue category, or social activity as a proxy for likelihood or value.
- Public disclosures can be incomplete, stale, or scoped differently. Prefer current official rules when sources conflict.
- Do not let fear of duplication override direct, in-scope evidence; preserve the conflict in the planning handoff.

## Scope And Privacy

Use only information permitted by the program and applicable law. Do not seek private reports, researcher identities, internal tickets, or non-public program information.

## Evidence And Handoff

Link each contextual statement to its source, date, and exact operational consequence. Hand off conflicts and ambiguity, not speculation about competition.

# Historical Evidence Review

## Purpose

Use relevant, permitted prior observations to formulate questions and identify evidence gaps. Historical material cannot establish current applicability, probability, severity, payout, or exploitability.

## Preconditions

- Records were collected lawfully and may be used for the current program.
- Each record identifies its source, time range, collection context, and known limitations.
- Sensitive details have been minimized or removed.

## Evidence-Led Method

1. Group prior observations by comparable feature, authorization context, and evidence type, not just issue label.
2. Identify recurring questions or controls worth checking in the current authorized scope.
3. For each proposed carry-over, cite the historical source and the current observation that makes it relevant.
4. Keep unsupported carry-overs as `defer` rather than promoting them.

## Interpretation And Controls

- Historical samples are often selective, incomplete, and affected by reporting or survivorship bias.
- Avoid averages, benchmarks, rankings, and extrapolated success claims unless the program has supplied a verified, authorized dataset and a qualified reviewer interprets it.
- Prefer recent, comparable, independently corroborated records. Note differences in asset, version, scope, and test conditions.

## Scope And Privacy

Do not reuse credentials, private report content, personal data, secrets, or evidence collected under another program's authorization. Respect retention and disclosure constraints.

## Evidence And Handoff

Pass only the minimal relevant citation, stated limitation, and current corroborating observation into the planning handoff.

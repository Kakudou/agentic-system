# Temporal Grammar

Time is information architecture.

## Three scales
Composition: opening → orientation → development → transformation → synthesis/climax → release → ending.
Scene: reason to enter → comprehension interval → emphasis → reason to leave/transform.
Element: position, scale, rotation, opacity, mask/clip, blur, color, path, camera relation, audio relation.

Not every work needs a climax; it may need a reveal, decision, loop, or stable final state.

## Determinism
Use current Remotion-approved frame-driven mechanisms. Visual state depends on current frame, fps/dimensions/duration, props, deterministic data, and seeded controlled inputs where needed. Avoid wall-clock time, uncontrolled timers, unstable randomness, or network timing.

## Motion semantics
Motion may express appearance, hierarchy, causality, transformation, passage, comparison, uncertainty, interruption, completion, recurrence. Different meanings deserve different timing. Do not give every entrance the same spring.

## Comprehension
After reveal, allow enough stable time. Increase for dense text, new diagrams, introductory audiences, unfamiliar grammar, comparisons. Decrease for repeated/known structures. When information density rises, reduce simultaneous motion/camera/audio complexity.

## Cuts/transitions
Cut for decisive semantic change or speed. Transition for continuity, morph, spatial preservation, or meaningful passage. Do not transition every cut.

## Camera
Camera changes conceptual perspective: zoom between abstractions, follow process, reveal hidden structure, reframe attention, traverse a model. Avoid constant floating motion.

## Loops
Make end/beginning compatible; verify first/last frames and audio loop points.

## Repeated motifs
Repeat only to create learnable grammar. Consistency should make later scenes easier to understand.

## Ending
End on resolved thesis, complete system, closed loop, clear action, deliberate open question, memorable symbol, or quiet Ōtsumi trace. Do not end merely because `durationInFrames` runs out.

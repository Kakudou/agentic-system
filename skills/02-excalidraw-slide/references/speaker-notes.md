# Speaker Notes

Use during storyboarding and implementation.

Speaker notes are a speaking interface, not a transcript dump and not projected content.

## Purpose

Notes should help the presenter:

- explain the visual
- preserve nuance
- remember evidence
- define terminology
- handle caveats
- ask the audience a question
- transition naturally
- manage time

They should reduce cognitive load while speaking.

## Default structure

Use only the sections that add value:

```text
Purpose
<why this slide exists>

Talk track
<what to explain in sequence>

Key terms
<definitions or exact vocabulary>

Example or analogy
<faithful illustration>

Evidence
<source, number, quotation context, or proof>

Caveat
<condition, uncertainty, limitation, or edge case>

Audience question
<prompt for reflection or interaction>

Transition
<link to the next slide>

Timing
<estimated speaking time>
```

Do not include empty headings.

## Talk-track discipline

The talk track should:

- follow the visual reading path
- begin with the takeaway
- explain only what is visible or necessary
- add nuance absent from the slide
- use natural spoken language
- identify where to pause or emphasize when useful
- avoid reading labels aloud mechanically

A useful sequence:

1. state the takeaway
2. orient the audience to the visual
3. explain the mechanism or evidence
4. name the implication
5. transition

## Length

Default:

- `100–220` words per ordinary slide
- up to approximately `300` words for dense technical or instructional slides
- less for statement, divider, or conclusion slides

When notes exceed the limit because the content has two arguments, split the slide.

Do not shrink notes text to force excess prose into the panel.

## Repetition test

Notes fail when they merely restate every projected word.

For each sentence, ask:

- Does this explain?
- Does this qualify?
- Does this provide evidence?
- Does this guide delivery?
- Does this transition?

If none apply, remove it.

## Audience adaptation

### Introductory audience

Notes may include:

- plain-language definitions
- analogy limitations
- likely misconceptions
- concrete examples
- reminders not to overstate the simplified model

### Specialist audience

Notes may include:

- exact mechanisms
- edge cases
- methodological detail
- alternative interpretations
- implementation constraints
- source provenance

### Mixed audience

Notes should:

- give the plain-language explanation first
- preserve the exact term
- provide optional specialist depth
- signal which details can be skipped for time

## Evidence and citations

Speaker notes are the preferred place for:

- source paths
- paper or chapter names
- dates
- methodological caveats
- exact values
- quotation provenance
- links when supported by the environment

Do not invent a citation.

If a claim is uncertain, write the uncertainty explicitly.

## Questions and interaction

Use an audience question only when it supports the learning or decision objective.

Good questions:

- test a distinction
- surface assumptions
- invite prediction before a reveal
- connect the concept to the audience's context
- prepare the next slide

Avoid generic prompts such as `Any thoughts?`

For training decks, include expected answer or facilitation guidance in the notes.

## Transitions

Every ordinary slide should have a natural transition.

A transition may:

- raise the next question
- reveal a missing layer
- move from model to example
- move from problem to response
- zoom in or out
- connect evidence to implication

Avoid abrupt `Next, we will discuss...` phrasing when a logical bridge is possible.

## Timing

Record realistic time:

- statement: `0:30–1:00`
- ordinary explanation: `1:00–2:00`
- dense diagram: `2:00–4:00`
- worked example: `3:00–8:00`
- exercise: based on actual interaction
- conclusion: `1:00–3:00`

When the total exceeds the target duration, remove or move material. Do not simply reduce every timing estimate.

## Notes-panel fitting

The panel has finite geometry.

Before delivery:

- text fits inside the notes panel
- headings and body remain readable
- no section is clipped
- line breaks are deliberate
- dense notes are shortened, not miniaturized
- notes correspond to the correct slide

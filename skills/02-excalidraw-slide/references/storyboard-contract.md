# Storyboard Contract

Use before any Excalidraw scene construction.

The storyboard is the approved content and narrative contract for the deck.

## Deck header

Record:

- **Working title**
- **Objective**
- **Target audience**
- **Audience expertise**
- **Presentation setting**
- **Estimated duration**
- **Planned slide count**
- **Central promise**
- **Narrative arc**
- **Style constraints**
- **Appendix strategy**
- **Source scope**
- **Assumptions and uncertainty**

## Slide record

Every slide must include:

```text
Slide NN — <takeaway title>

Narrative role:
Audience objective:
Source-backed content:
Primary visual grammar:
Local layout mixture:
Key labels/keywords:
Evidence or source note:
Speaker-note objective:
Transition in:
Transition out:
Estimated time:
Appendix status:
```

### Takeaway title

A takeaway title states what the audience should understand.

Good:

- `The queue protects throughput by absorbing burst traffic`
- `Three factions control the city's information economy`
- `Temperature shifts the equilibrium, not the reaction identity`

Weak:

- `Queues`
- `Factions`
- `Temperature`

Use a topic title only for orientation, section division, glossary, or appendix reference.

### Narrative role

Choose one primary role:

- orient
- provoke
- define
- explain
- compare
- demonstrate
- reveal
- connect
- summarize
- transition
- challenge
- instruct
- conclude
- call to action

Do not assign several roles to hide an unfocused slide.

### Audience objective

State what changes in the audience after the slide:

- can name
- can distinguish
- can explain
- can trace
- can predict
- can choose
- can perform
- can challenge
- can remember

### Source-backed content

List only the facts, concepts, relationships, examples, and qualifiers required on the slide.

Distinguish:

- projected content
- speaker-note content
- optional appendix detail

The storyboard must not rely on future invention during design.

### Visual grammar

Select the structure that communicates the takeaway:

- assertion
- comparison
- timeline
- flow
- architecture
- hierarchy
- map
- annotated object
- sequence
- taxonomy
- before/after
- process
- quote with context
- exercise
- synthesis
- deliberate hybrid

Do not select a grammar because it creates visual variety.

### Key labels

List exact terms that should appear.

Keep terminology stable across slides.

### Evidence note

Record source path, section, citation identifier, or provenance when required.

Do not clutter the projected slide with full provenance unless the audience must see it.

### Transitions

`Transition in` explains why this slide follows the previous one.

`Transition out` creates the next question or move.

Slide 1 may use `Opening`.

The final slide may use `Resolution`.

### Timing

Estimate realistic speaking time from complexity.

Do not assign 30 seconds to a slide that requires explaining a dense architecture.

## Slide uniqueness test

Before approval, reject or merge a slide when:

- its takeaway duplicates another
- it exists only to hold leftover facts
- the notes contain the actual point while the slide is generic
- it introduces a concept never used again
- it breaks the narrative without a transition
- it requires unreadably dense projected content
- it has no audience objective

## Split test

Split a slide when:

- it contains two independent takeaways
- it requires two unrelated primary visual grammars
- the audience must understand one half before the other
- projected text becomes prose-heavy
- the diagram cannot remain legible
- notes exceed approximately 300 words because two arguments are competing

## Merge test

Merge slides when:

- one is only a definition for the next
- each is too weak to justify a separate visual beat
- they share the same audience objective
- the transition adds no narrative value
- a comparison is clearer side by side

## Section structure

Use section dividers only when:

- the deck changes question, scale, phase, or mode
- the audience benefits from orientation
- the divider performs a narrative reset

A divider should contain a meaningful promise, not merely a chapter number.

## Appendix records

Mark appendix slides explicitly.

Appendix slides may be excluded from the main speaking-time estimate unless likely to be presented.

They must still preserve deck style and speaker notes where useful.

## Approval package

Present the storyboard in a scan-friendly form.

For a large deck, group slides by section but retain every slide record.

The user must be able to:

- inspect the complete narrative
- see what each slide will accomplish
- identify missing or unnecessary content
- understand the proposed visual grammar
- approve without imagining unstated material

# Audience Calibration

Use during `CALIBRATE`.

A presentation is not accurate merely because its source facts are correct. It must also use the right abstraction, vocabulary, pace, and evidence for the people receiving it.

## Audience profile

Determine:

| Dimension | Question |
|---|---|
| Role | What do they do in relation to this subject? |
| Expertise | What can they already understand without explanation? |
| Motivation | Why should they care? |
| Decision | What should they decide, remember, or do afterward? |
| Risk | What misunderstanding would be costly? |
| Context | Classroom, workshop, conference, briefing, pitch, onboarding, or self-study? |
| Language | Which terms are familiar, unfamiliar, overloaded, or politically sensitive? |
| Time | How much attention and speaking time are available? |

Do not infer expertise only from job title.

## Calibration levels

### Introductory

Use when the audience is new to the subject.

Projected slides should:

- establish relevance quickly
- use concrete examples before abstraction
- define essential terminology
- show one mechanism at a time
- prefer visual comparisons and sequences
- avoid unexplained notation
- keep exceptions in notes until the main model is stable

Speaker notes should preserve nuance and warn against oversimplification.

### Working knowledge

Use when the audience knows the domain but not this specific topic.

Projected slides may:

- use ordinary domain vocabulary
- move faster through foundations
- emphasize distinctions, mechanisms, and practical consequences
- compare alternatives
- show operational flows
- include moderate technical detail

Speaker notes should supply edge cases, source evidence, and implementation nuance.

### Expert

Use when the audience can evaluate precision.

Projected slides may:

- use exact terminology and notation
- foreground constraints, tradeoffs, exceptions, and evidence
- show complex relationships when they serve the objective
- omit basic definitions
- distinguish models, assumptions, and disputed claims

Do not inflate density merely because the audience is expert. Experts still benefit from structure.

### Mixed

Design the projected slide for the lowest necessary entry level while preserving expert precision through:

- compact secondary labels
- speaker notes
- appendix slides
- optional detail frames
- clearly marked assumptions
- exact terminology after a plain-language anchor

Do not alternate randomly between beginner and expert language.

## Simplification without corruption

Simplification may:

- remove nonessential detail
- replace jargon with an accurate plain-language phrase
- isolate one causal step
- use a faithful analogy
- defer an exception to notes
- combine equivalent cases

Simplification may not:

- reverse causality
- erase uncertainty
- imply universality
- remove a condition that changes the result
- replace a technical term with a misleading everyday meaning
- invent a metaphor that suggests the wrong structure
- hide material risk

## Jargon and buzzwords

Use a term when at least one is true:

- the source uses it as a precise concept
- the audience must learn it
- it is standard vocabulary for the target group
- it improves brevity without damaging clarity

Do not use a term merely because it sounds impressive.

At first use:

- define it plainly
- show its role
- use the exact term consistently afterward

If a term is overloaded, state which meaning applies.

## Analogy test

An analogy is acceptable only when:

- the mapped relationships are explicit
- the analogy preserves the important direction or mechanism
- the limitation is known
- it reduces cognitive load

Reject the analogy when it introduces more exceptions than clarity.

For technical audiences, label analogy as analogy.

## Audience-action alignment

Every deck should support one primary audience outcome:

- understand
- remember
- compare
- decide
- perform
- troubleshoot
- discuss
- teach
- change behavior

A slide that does not serve the selected outcome must justify its presence.

## Calibration audit

Before storyboarding, confirm:

- vocabulary matches expertise
- examples match audience context
- slide pace matches duration
- required background is supplied
- unnecessary background is removed
- caveats are placed appropriately
- technical precision is preserved
- the intended action is explicit

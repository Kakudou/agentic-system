# Scope And Admitted Evidence

## Purpose

Decide whether supplied material is sufficient and authorized for hypothesis generation.

## Preconditions

Require written authorization, in-scope assets, exclusions, permitted handling constraints, and a named handoff recipient.

## Evidence-Led Method

Admit only observations that identify their source, asset, collection context, and time. Mark evidence as direct, indirect, or unverified. Return a scope question when the asset, authorization, or provenance cannot be established.

## Interpretation And Uncertainty

Authorization can be narrower than a program description, and observations can be stale or copied without context. Missing evidence is not negative evidence.

## False-Positive And Bias Controls

Do not fill scope gaps from assumptions, related domains, historical reports, or technology clues. Keep observation, interpretation, and conclusion separate.

## Scope And Privacy Limits

Honor all exclusions, testing windows, account restrictions, data minimization duties, and third-party boundaries. Do not retain unnecessary personal, credential, or secret material.

## Evidence And Handoff

Record the authorization reference, admitted assets, exclusions, evidence inventory, unresolved questions, and stop decision in the checklist.

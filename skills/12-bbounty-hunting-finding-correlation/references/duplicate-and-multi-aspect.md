# Duplicate And Multi-Aspect Distinction

## Purpose And Preconditions

Distinguish potentially redundant observations from separate aspects of the same surface without discarding evidence. All items must already be admitted.

## Evidence-Led Method

1. Compare affected surface, observable behavior, required conditions, control boundary, impact evidence, and evidence locator.
2. Mark a duplicate candidate only when the observations describe the same underlying instance under materially equivalent conditions. Retain all independent evidence records.
3. Mark a multi-aspect candidate when one surface has distinct, independently evidenced behaviors, impacts, or affected parties. List each aspect separately.
4. When a shared cause or equivalence is not evidenced, classify the relationship as unresolved rather than duplicate or multi-aspect.
5. Defer any report-consolidation choice to the authorized report owner and program policy.

## Interpretation And Uncertainty

Different tools finding the same behavior may be duplicate evidence, not necessarily duplicate findings. Different endpoints can be distinct findings even when they appear similar. Multiple aspects do not prove that impacts combine, chain, or increase severity.

## Controls

- Do not deduplicate by hashes, labels, titles, or endpoint strings alone.
- Preserve the strongest and contradictory evidence rather than selecting a preferred narrative.
- Avoid combining user, tenant, or account data across findings; use redacted references.
- Treat program-specific duplicate rules as the final classification authority.

## Evidence And Handoff

Hand off member IDs, compared fields, duplicate or multi-aspect rationale, retained evidence locators, disagreements, and the decision owner. Clearly identify items that remain separately reportable pending review.

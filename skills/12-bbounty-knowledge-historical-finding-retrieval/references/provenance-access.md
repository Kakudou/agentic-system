# Provenance And Access Boundaries

## Purpose And Preconditions

Use when recording a historical source or derived statement. Require a source reference, origin or publisher where known, access classification, collection context, permitted recipient class, and any transformation or redaction history.

## Documentation Methodology

Keep a stable reference to the source rather than copying it wholesale. Distinguish direct source statements, curator summaries, and analyst interpretations. Note whether provenance is verified, partial, unknown, or restricted, and whether access is authorized, limited, or unavailable. Preserve the source's date and stated scope without silently normalizing them to the present.

## Privacy And False-Claim Controls

Do not treat possession as permission to retain, share, or rely on a source. Do not cite inaccessible material as independently verified. Never move restricted excerpts into general notes, and do not disclose credentials, personal data, internal identifiers, or customer material in provenance fields.

## Evidence And Handoff

Include provenance and access status beside every source-derived claim. Send restricted references only through the approved channel to authorized recipients. Stop and escalate conflicting provenance, suspected unauthorized access, or a request to broaden distribution.

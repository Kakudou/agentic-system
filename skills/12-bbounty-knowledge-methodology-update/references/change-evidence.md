# Change Proposal And Evidence Admission

## Purpose

Turn a requested methodology change into a bounded, reviewable proposal. Admission determines whether a source may support a documentation claim; it does not authorize operational activity or adoption.

## Preconditions

- Identify the methodology document, current version, and accountable owner.
- Record the requested outcome and what is explicitly out of scope.
- Retain a stable source locator for every submitted observation.

## Documentation Methodology

1. Assign a proposal ID and state one atomic change claim.
2. Capture each source verbatim enough to preserve its meaning, with author/origin, date, and locator.
3. Classify evidence as direct observation, approved policy/requirement, retrospective finding, or contextual reference.
4. Admit only evidence relevant to the named claim. Record the admission decision and the reason for exclusion when evidence is not admitted.
5. Separate facts, interpretation, and proposed wording. A proposal may cite an interpretation but must label it as such.

## Uncertainty And Safety Controls

- Unverifiable provenance, ambiguous scope, or altered source material is not admitted as supporting evidence.
- One result, anecdote, or external trend may motivate review but cannot establish a broad rule.
- Remove sensitive target identifiers and report details unless the designated owner has explicitly authorized their inclusion.
- Missing evidence is a review gap, not a reason to invent a rationale.

## Evidence And Handoff

Attach the completed [evidence/applicability matrix](../assets/evidence-applicability-matrix.md) to the proposal. Hand off the source inventory, admission decisions, excluded evidence, and open questions to the reviewer.

## Authoritative Sources

- The identified current methodology and its controlled version history
- Written program requirements or policy notices from their authoritative issuer
- Original, access-controlled finding or retrospective records

# Admissible Evidence And Scope

## Purpose

Establish which existing records may support a surface review and when review must stop.

## Preconditions

- Written authorization identifies the approved assets, dates or engagement window, evidence sources, handling rules, and escalation contact.
- Every proposed record has an attributable source and collection context.

## Evidence-Led Methodology

Admit only program-provided materials, approved passive records, or observations produced under the recorded authorization. For each record, retain its source reference, timestamp, collection context, scope match, and redaction status. Exclude unattributed claims, material outside the approved window, and records whose ownership or scope cannot be established.

## Interpretation

Admissibility establishes that a record can be reviewed. It does not establish completeness, ownership, current availability, security relevance, or permission for further activity.

## Bias And False-Positive Controls

- Do not treat search snippets, stale inventories, third-party references, or framework markers as ownership proof.
- Keep source text, analyst summary, and unresolved interpretation distinct.
- Stop rather than broaden scope when a record points to an unapproved asset or contains sensitive material.

## Privacy And Scope Limits

Retain the minimum evidence needed for traceability. Redact credentials, personal data, tokens, internal identifiers, and unnecessary content. Do not collect new evidence, replay activity, authenticate, or contact systems through this skill.

## Evidence And Handoff

Hand off the authorization reference, admissible source list, excluded records with reason, redactions, scope gaps, and any decision needed to proceed.

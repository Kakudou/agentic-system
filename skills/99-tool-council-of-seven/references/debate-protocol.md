# Debate Protocol

Cross-fire exists to test material claims, not to generate more noise.

## When to Cross-Fire

Use it when:

- two lenses make incompatible claims about the same material point;
- severity differs materially because of a factual or contractual disagreement;
- one finding challenges a premise another lens relies on.

Skip it when findings are compatible or the disagreement is merely stylistic.

## Neutral Briefing

Give each side:

- the exact disputed claim;
- the opposing claim;
- relevant evidence already cited;
- the submission context and constraints.

Do not tell either side which claim is preferred.

## Cross-Fire Output

Each side returns:

- what it accepts from the opposing evidence;
- what remains disputed;
- any stronger evidence or reasoning;
- whether its original severity/confidence changes.

Do not open unrelated review topics.

## Synthesis

After cross-fire, the Synthesizer receives both positions and the evidence. It applies the conflict-resolution rules and preserves unresolved disagreement when the evidence remains insufficient.

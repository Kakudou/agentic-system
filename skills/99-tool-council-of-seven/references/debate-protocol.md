# Debate Protocol

## Round 1 — Independent Critique

All seven seats receive the same submission packet, but no other seat's output.

Each seat returns:
- 1–5 findings,
- severity for each finding,
- concrete reference/evidence,
- consequence,
- confidence,
- one-sentence overall impression.

Keep each seat concise. The point is diversity of judgment, not essay volume.

## Neutral Briefing

After Round 1, the orchestrator summarizes findings without deciding who is correct.

Include:
- shared conclusions,
- unique material findings,
- direct contradictions,
- evidence cited,
- unresolved unknowns.

Do not include rhetorical framing such as “Keima correctly observed...” before cross-fire.

## Round 2 — Cross-Fire

### Dynamic pairing rule

Pair the seats that materially contradict each other on the same claim whenever possible.

Examples:
- Keima says retries are required for reliability; Fuhyō says retries are needless complexity.
- Hisha says onboarding is unusably dense; Kinshō says further UX work has poor ROI.
- Kakugyō says a boundary is necessary; Fuhyō says it is abstraction overhead.

### Default pairings

When no direct contradiction exists but debate is still useful:
- Keima ↔ Kakugyō
- Fuhyō ↔ Kyōsha
- Hisha ↔ Kinshō

Ginshō does not need a paired opponent; it observes the debate.

### Cross-fire rules

- Challenge the opponent's **claim and evidence**, not the persona.
- Defend or revise your own claim.
- Concede when the opponent has stronger evidence.
- Do not open an unrelated review thread.
- Keep the response compact.

## Round 2 skip conditions

Skip Cross-Fire when:
- the input is too small for debate to add value,
- Round 1 is materially unanimous,
- all disagreements are merely stylistic preferences with no consequence,
- there is no evidence available to distinguish the competing claims.

If skipped, state why.

## Round 3 — Alternatives

Each seat proposes exactly one implementable improvement, alternative, mitigation, or simplification based on the debate.

An alternative should state:
- what changes,
- why it addresses a material concern,
- what tradeoff it introduces.

Do not silently convert alternatives into requirements.

## Synthesis

Ginshō reads:
- the original submission packet,
- all Round 1 findings,
- the neutral briefing,
- all Cross-Fire responses,
- all alternatives.

Ginshō then applies `references/conflict-resolution.md` and produces the Council Report.

# Scope And Identity Controls

## Purpose

Define what may be observed before collecting any passive source material. Authorization for one domain, brand, repository, or subsidiary does not establish authorization for another.

## Preconditions

- Retain the engagement's written scope, rules, reporting path, and dates.
- Normalize only the identities explicitly named in scope: domains, organizations, repository organizations, products, or legal entities.
- Record exclusions, third-party providers, and uncertainty as constraints rather than expanding scope.

## Passive Authorized Method

Compare a source's stated identity to the authorized identity set. Record the relationship evidence, such as an official organization profile, published ownership statement, or program scope statement. Read only public third-party metadata or public pages; do not resolve names, browse target services, authenticate, or test discovered assets.

## Interpretation And Controls

- A naming similarity, shared hosting provider, certificate co-occurrence, or employee mention is not ownership proof.
- Treat subsidiaries, acquired brands, customer domains, and cloud tenant names as out of scope until explicitly included.
- A public repository contributor does not prove that the repository or its contents are target-owned.

## Privacy And Scope Limits

Collect organization-level facts only. Avoid personal profiles, contact details, employee enumeration, and data unrelated to an in-scope claim. Stop and escalate when public material appears to expose credentials, tokens, personal data, or target content that is not necessary to establish scope.

## Evidence And Handoff

For each claim, retain the authorization reference, source URL, observed date, identity mapping, and confidence. Handoff ambiguous ownership as an unverified hypothesis; do not recommend interaction with it.

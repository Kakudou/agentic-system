# Cypher — combat, armor, Pools, damage and rest

## Combat

A PC attacks by rolling a **d20 against a difficulty based on the foe's level**, adjusted by relevant skill, assets, Effort and circumstance. For an opponent attack, **the player rolls defense** against the foe's level rather than GM rolling to hit. The defense task uses a suitable stat (commonly Speed, sometimes Might/Intellect depending on attack). NPCs are normally represented by **level**, **health**, **damage**, **Armor**, and specific level adjustments; NPCs do not spend Effort.

Most attacks deal a fixed damage amount (commonly **light weapon 2**, **medium weapon 4**, **heavy weapon 6**), with weapon practice and ability restrictions according to type/sheet. Apply damage bonuses from natural special rolls, Effort spent specifically on damage (+3 per level of damage Effort), effects and weaknesses if applicable. A player can generally assign Effort toward easier hit or extra damage, constrained by maximum Effort and resource cost. **Do not turn every successful hit into a separate damage die.**

**Armor** subtracts from incoming damage before Pool/health reduction; light physical armor generally 1, medium 2, heavy 3. Add specifically applicable armor abilities/tech as allowed; ignore physical Armor against damage types (for example some Speed/Intellect/ambient damage) that explicitly bypass it. Record penalties to Speed Pool when wearing restrictive armor if character abilities don't eliminate them. An NPC's base **health normally equals level × 3** unless its stat block says otherwise.

## PC health and damage track

Damage normally reduces **Might Pool first** unless the source says Speed or Intellect damage; attacks can also reduce another Pool or inflict special states. A Pool cannot go below zero: additional ordinary damage may roll into another Pool according to the official damage allocation; apply any special effects. Every time a **new Pool reaches 0** from damage, move one step down the track:

`hale → impaired → debilitated → dead`.

- **Hale**: character is in normal condition.
- **Impaired**: Effort costs +1 **per level**; 19/20 no longer grant ordinary minor/major effects; special attack rolls 17+ deal just +1 damage.
- **Debilitated**: can normally only move an immediate distance (crawl), no other normal actions; if Speed Pool is 0 cannot move.
- **Dead**: out of play subject to table's established return-to-play policy.

Some special effects, poison, disease, and severe trauma shift the track directly. NPCs normally have health rather than PC Pools/tracks. Check exceptional damage types before claiming death.

## Recovery

A recovery roll is **1d6 + PC tier**, allocated among missing Pool points up to current maxima, on eligible rest. Four progressively longer recovery opportunities per daily cycle:

| Recovery roll # | Minimum rest |
|---|---|
| 1 | One action |
| 2 | Ten minutes |
| 3 | One hour |
| 4 | Ten hours |

When a Pool returns from 0 to ≥1 by recovery, the track can improve one step as rules permit. Track which recovery rolls have already been used that day and any bonus to recovery. Natural living/medical conditions and explicit abilities can modify recovery. Do not reset daily uses merely due to a chat interruption.

## Hazards and combat storytelling

Assign real danger and scene-specific levels: cover, darkness, distance, footing, pressure, morale and opportunities to withdraw matter. Armor, Pool attrition and one-action economy work underneath the narrator's text. Describe injury specifically rather than treating losing 4 Might as a purely abstract number; keep numeric details in the ledger.

# Scope and Observation Boundaries

## Purpose and Preconditions

Translate engagement authorization into a narrow content-observation boundary. Require the current program policy or owner approval, exact approved targets and paths, allowed observation methods, time and rate limits, data-handling rules, and a stop contact.

## Passive or Approved Low-Impact Methodology

Begin with approved entry points and normal navigation. Observe only content visibly linked, embedded, or otherwise referenced by an approved page or passive source. Record external destinations and redirects without following them unless independently authorized. An expressly approved low-impact revisit may confirm a material observation; no guessing, enumeration, or automated discovery is permitted.

## Interpretation

A visible link or resource reference establishes provenance, not scope. Treat alternate hosts, ports, identity services, APIs, preview environments, downloads, and archived material as `needs-review` unless explicitly covered. Robots directives and public availability do not expand authorization.

## False-Positive and Scope Controls

Keep source, effective target, access context, and collection time together. Do not submit forms, invoke actions, create accounts, authenticate without approval, or access content that might alter state. Stop for sensitive material, an unclear boundary, rate limiting, instability, or unexpected access changes.

## Evidence and Handoff

Record the authorization reference, boundary decision, navigation source, target locator, request count, and stop reason. Use the [scope/stop checklist](../assets/scope-stop-checklist.md). Escalate boundary questions to the engagement owner rather than resolving them through further requests.
